# Level 3 procedure

Read this only after the user has said "level 3" (or "level three") about a decision that clears the gate in SKILL.md — or has been asked about a failed gate and chosen this level anyway. Show the gate under the Mode line as `Gate: important ✓/✗ · composite ✓/✗`, whichever way it went. Level 2 remains the right tool for a single atomic choice; this level earns its cost when the decision is really several decisions wearing one name.

The goal is **accuracy, not volume**. The mechanisms, in order of leverage: ground every load-bearing claim in a checked fact; analyze each component decision in isolation; recompose as whole strategies — coherent paths through the components — and compare those, rather than assembling locally best calls; derive the top-level answer twice by independent routes, treating agreement as earned confidence and disagreement as the finding; attack the composed plan before shipping it. Spend there. Independent derivations that agree are a stronger accuracy signal than agents debating — cross-talk breeds conformity — which is why every isolation rule below exists.

**Phase 0 — Decompose, then attack the map.** Split the decision into its component decisions: the separate choices that each have real options and could each go differently. Three to six. More than six means the question is a program, not a decision — say so and scope with the user. For each component, one line: the problem it exists to solve. Then mark the structure: the load-bearing component (if it resolves one way the rest are moot — analyze it first and feed its answer to the others as a constraint) and any coupled pairs (one's answer changes the other's options). Spend one check attacking the map itself before proceeding — a missing component or a false split is the error every later phase inherits.

Then build the table. For each component, generate its options — step 2's generator list plus whatever the facts suggest, up to step 2's ceiling — and screen them as step 2 says: drop what breaks a hard constraint, what another option dominates on every lens, and what shares another option's premise (merge those); record each discard in a line with its reason so it can be revived. The survivors are the component's row in the table: usually two to four, occasionally more when the component genuinely offers more shapes that differ in kind. Then mark the bars: every pair of options from different components that cannot both be chosen (one forbids the other, a rule or a dependency makes them incompatible), each with the fact that bars it. The bars are what make a composed strategy coherent or not, and finding them here is far cheaper than finding them in Phase 4.

**Phase 1 — Ground each component.** Research per component, not in bulk: the actual clause, the real price at your volume, the committed date, each taken from the system of record for that kind of fact. Roughly two to four lookups per component, keeping about a third of the whole budget back for Phase 4's aimed round; list what you skipped. Where a reference class exists — earlier decisions of this kind, here or elsewhere, with known outcomes — spend one of the component's lookups on its base rate, because the outside view is the cheapest correction for a case that reads as special. When two sources disagree, the record-level one outranks the summary and the conflict is written down, not silently resolved. Where a component's record is silent, borrow from its neighbors: the other components, earlier decisions of the same kind, a case that went the other way, each with its similarity check. For each component also build its inferred case, as level-2.md's Phase 0 describes: the mechanism chain per option, its second-order implications, weakest link and falsifier, written whether or not the lookups confirmed it, so a component with a silent record still has a case to analyze. Mark every number and load-bearing claim measured, estimated or inferred (chain and weakest link written out, per `reasoning.md`) — Phase 4 sensitivity-tests the estimates and flips the inferred links, so mark honestly.

**Phase 2 — Analyze each component in isolation.** One compact stress-test per component: its row of the table, the two always-on lenses plus the one or two profile lenses that bite hardest on it, and a verdict. Each returns: the problem, the call, the inferred chain the call rests on (or that it rests on measured facts), the plan of action for that component, the strongest objection to its own call, and the single fact that would flip it. Isolation is the level 2 discipline applied per component: each analysis sees the options, the grounded facts, and the Phase 0 constraints — never another component's conclusion, never the synthesis. With subagents, one per component, spawned in a single message. Without them, approximate with files exactly as level-2.md describes — one file per component, written before any synthesis — and label it approximated.

**Phase 3 — Recompose as strategies, then derive twice, independently.** A strategy is one coherent path through the table: one option per component, crossing no bar. First synthesis: build the strategies worth comparing — the one assembled from the component calls in dependency order (if it crosses a bar, say so and exclude it; that is a finding, not a patch to make quietly), plus the two or three other coherent paths that differ from it in kind, each named by its theme (a sequence that defers the hard-to-reverse component until a named fact is known is almost always one of them). Compare the strategies through the two always-on lenses and the two profile lenses that bite hardest, as whole plans, because a component call that was best in isolation often loses inside the strategy it forces. Pick one and write the master plan of action — the component plans sequenced, with owners and dates where they exist. Second derivation: answer the top-level question again from only the table, the bars and the grounded facts — including the inferred-case section, which it may reason from directly and extend — blind to the component verdicts and the first synthesis — a fresh subagent, or a fresh file-isolated section. A second derivation that reaches the same call by a different chain is stronger agreement than one that reuses the first chain; say which happened. Agreement between the two is earned confidence; disagreement is the finding — name it and resolve by reasoning, never by preferring the longer analysis or splitting the difference.

**Phase 4 — Accuracy checks on the composed plan.** Three, in order:

- *Interaction check.* Locally best calls can compose into an incoherent whole — two components that each chose "defer" can leave nothing happening. Also ask what each component's facts imply for the others that no component stated. Walk the master plan end to end once as a story and name any step that only makes sense if some component had gone the other way.
- *Sensitivity flips.* When the record holds the numbers, compute: a few lines of code with every input marked (`scripts/sensitivity_sweep.py`, or the equivalent), giving the base case, the break-even on the dominant uncertainty, and a ranked sweep of each input across its plausible range — a threshold eyeballed by hand drops the structure (the share affected, the base rate, the interaction with a second quantity) and is usually wrong by enough to matter. Where there are no numbers, take the two or three estimates the decision leans on hardest, double and halve each, and say plainly whether the top-level call survives. Either way, do the same for the two or three inferred chains it leans on hardest: assume the weakest link fails and say whether the call survives. The top-ranked uncertainty is the research target: spend the held-back third of the lookup budget on it now, and on any rule the master plan depends on that has not been read, then restate the call and show the round as an **Aimed round** block — target, finding, call changed or unchanged. A call that still flips on one soft number or one unchecked link after that is a research task, not a decision — name the number to nail down or the link to test, and stop there if that is the honest state; where the number can be had by a small test, give the test with its proceed and stop thresholds taken from the model.
- *Adversarial pass.* As at level 2: hand the plan and its inferred links, not the reasoning, to one agent or file-isolated section and have it argue the plan fails, weakest link first. Rule on every objection; keep what survives.

**Output.** The Mode line; the decomposition map with the load-bearing component and the bars marked; the screened-out options in a line each; one tight block per component — problem, call, plan of action, flip condition; the strategies compared, one line each with why the chosen one won; the composed decision and the master plan; success and abandon lines for the whole, with thresholds from the model where there is one; the inference share for the composed call; the sensitivity results and what the aimed round changed; and what was checked against what was skipped, closing with `Inference share:` and `Spend:` (lookups used against the budget, grounding and aimed round separately, and the count of agent calls or isolated sections run) as the last two lines. Keep it under about 1,400 words — past that, confidence grows but accuracy does not — by moving detail into the delivered files, never by dropping the fixed lines.

### Cost policy

Bound spend by structure, not model label: roughly two calls per component plus five — grounding and analysis per component, one strategy comparison, one second derivation, one adversarial pass — plus a model run when numbers exist, each with a tight prompt, never the whole conversation. Eleven to sixteen calls for a four-component decision. If the decision turns out atomic — midway, or because the user chose this level past the gate — do not invent components: treat the whole decision as one component and keep every accuracy mechanism that still applies (the grounding, the second independent derivation, the sensitivity flips, the adversarial pass); only the interaction check and the strategy table fall away — an atomic decision's strategies are simply its options. When escalating from a finished level 2, reuse its grounding and options; do not redo them. In the no-subagent file approximation, write Phase 3's second derivation immediately after the facts file, before any component analysis, so later conclusions cannot leak backward into it.
