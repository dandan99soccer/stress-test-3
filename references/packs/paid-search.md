# Paid-search pack, part one — forms, outcomes, constraints first, the account reference, sizing, the brief

Read with `paid-search-b.md` (the reading order, the evidence checklist with clocks, base rates, linked quantities, data rules, tools, open items; sections 10–17) and the level 6 files when the decision's object is a search-advertising account or part of one (Google Ads, Microsoft Advertising): a budget, a bid strategy or its target, a campaign or ad-group structure, keywords or match types, a landing page, a brand-term policy, or the weight of one campaign in an account's budget. `Pack: paid-search` on the Mode line. **Drafted 3 Nov 2026 and untested on a real decision**: its first real run is its test, and that run records in section 17 what it changed here (`packs/README.md`, "Size and style"). Not this pack: organic ranking (a how-to, not a decision of this kind); display, video or social campaigns (a different auction and attribution clock — `Pack: none (routes)` until a pack exists); an ad-copy edit below the stakes floor.

## 1. Name and trigger

`paid-search`, chosen in Phase 0 when the object is one of those above. Scripts: `sensitivity_sweep.py` for the break-evens, `forecast_ledger.py` for the rows, `propagate.py` where section 13 applies; no pack-specific script yet (section 16).

## 2. Decision forms

Raise, hold, cut or reallocate a budget; change a bid strategy or its target (a cost-per-acquisition or return-on-ad-spend target, maximize conversions, manual bidding); restructure (split, merge, pause, a new campaign type); add or remove keywords, match types or negatives; change a landing page; bid or not on brand terms; when (now, or after a conversion-count milestone). **Hold is always a form with full forecasts and comes first on every table**; a change must beat it by a printed hurdle: the learning cost (conversions forgone while the strategy re-learns, section 5), the read delay (the window before the effect can be read, section 15), and the owner's change-record haircut (section 12). Each form is decided by four quantities: the constraint-only call (4), the incremental cost per acquisition or return against hold at the horizon (7), the dollars per period at risk (7) and the edge verdict (4).

## 3. Outcome variables and the scoring rule

- **Primary row**: conversions, or conversion value, per reporting week at the account's own conversion definition, at **4 and 13 weeks** after the change takes effect, as five quantiles, scored by pinball loss against the **account reference** of section 5 stored in `ref_value` when the row is made and never recomputed. Brand and non-brand rows separately when the account splits them, because they resolve by different mechanisms (brand: substitution from organic; non-brand: the auction).
- **Efficiency rows**: cost per acquisition or return on ad spend at the same horizons, five quantiles.
- **Event rows** where the decision needs them: P(cost per acquisition above the kill level for two consecutive weeks within 13 weeks); P(the campaign leaves its learning state within 14 days); P(monthly spend within ±10% of budget); P(a lift test, where one runs, reads incrementality below the pre-registered floor).
- **Resolution source**: the platform's report read after the conversion window has closed (section 15), from the account's reporting connector or export; value rows resolve from the system the account imports offline conversions from.
- **n is independent clusters**: date × account × non-overlapping horizon; every campaign changed in one account in one week is one cluster. Skill at one account's decision rate is not establishable inside a year; the record's job is catching miscalibration, and the packet says so.

## 4. Constraints first

Before any research, Phase 0 prints the **constraint-only call**: the budget cap and the pacing rule (the platform may spend up to about twice the average daily budget on a day and no more than the monthly limit, so a daily budget is a monthly cap — Google Ads help, "About average daily budgets"; not re-read for this draft, verify on the first run); policy constraints (what the account may not advertise; brand-term agreements with partners); the conversion floor the bid strategy needs (section 5); the kill level the owner already holds (a cost-per-acquisition ceiling, a return floor); the approval the change needs. The fixed line reads `Constraint call: <change> — direction fixed by <cap | pacing | conversion floor | policy | approval> | open`. When the constraints fix the direction, the packet's first line after the call says so and the pass runs the fast form.

Research is then **one edge hunt**, at most five questions, with a bar pre-registered before the first lookup: **an edge counts only if it moves the monthly dollars at risk by at least 10%, moves the efficiency forecast across the kill level, or flips the call**; a finding that clears none goes in the ledger and changes nothing. No graded edge means no discretionary change: the call reads "hold; constraints only".

## 5. Market and crowd benchmarks

- **The account's own trailing record is the reference forecaster.** There is no traded price in this domain; the reference is the last 13 weeks of the row's quantity, per week, with the prior year's same weeks as the seasonal adjustment when the account has one. Transform: the median and the 5/25/75/95 percentiles of the weekly values, widened by the conversion-lag haircut of section 15.
- **Industry benchmarks** (click-through rate, cost per click, conversion rate, cost per lead by industry) are published yearly by several vendors and **disagree by about 2× on the same metric and year** (compiled 3 Nov 2026 from the vendor reports in section 17: the WordStream-based figures put the all-industry search conversion rate near 7–8% and the cost per lead near $65–70; other compilations near 4–6% and $75; legal is the costliest vertical in all of them), because each measures a different account mix. Role: direction only, never a trigger. A benchmark becomes a row's prior only when the account has fewer than 13 weeks of its own record, and then marked `estimated` with the vendor and year.
- **The platform's own numbers** — a bid-strategy simulator, a budget recommendation, a keyword-planner estimate — are the seller's: an upper bound on the upside, never the reference; recorded in the ledger with forecaster `platform`.
- **Learning period.** The platform's documentation says a bid-strategy change, a setting change or a large composition change puts a campaign into a learning state and recommends about two weeks before judging it or changing it again (Google Ads help, "Learning and review periods" and "Duration of the learning period", read through search excerpts 3 Nov 2026 — grade S until the pages are read in full). A Google product manager said in 2026 that target and budget changes on a steady campaign do not restart learning (trade-press report, S). Pack rule: a change that touches the bid strategy prices a two-week learning cost against hold; a budget or target change prices a one-week read delay; the third-party "20–30% budget change" thresholds are unsourced and not used.
- **Conversion floor.** The documentation ties automated bidding to recent conversion volume; the pack treats about 30 conversions in 30 days per campaign as the floor below which a target-based strategy is expected to underperform — `estimated` (vendor guidance, not measured) until the account's own record gives its number.

## 6. The prior model

The funnel per campaign: impressions × click-through rate × conversion rate × value per conversion, with cost = clicks × cost per click. Three to six drivers per decision, each a row: `driver | trailing 13-week value (dispersion, trend) | ours | platform's figure | grade | resolves when`. The two drivers with the largest swing on the primary row are the load-bearing numbers, named on the Mode line. For a budget change they are almost always the **marginal** cost per click (the auction's response to more spend is convex: the next dollar buys fewer clicks than the last) and the **marginal** conversion rate (the next click converts worse than the average); the pack's rule is that **a budget forecast at average cost per acquisition is wrong by construction**, and the model carries a marginal-cost curve, even one marked `estimated` from the account's last two budget changes. Completeness is measured against the driver list.

## 7. Sizing and the objective

The call protects **incremental conversions (or value) net of cost at the horizon, paired against hold** — not platform-reported conversions, which include conversions that would have happened anyway (brand searches, returning customers). The pack prices a **cannibalization haircut** on brand-term rows. The one large field experiment (eBay; Blake, Nosko and Tadelis, *Econometrica* 83(1), 2015, M — primary known, not re-read for this draft) found brand-keyword ads with no measurable short-term effect for a brand whose organic listing ranked first; later studies on other brands (Edmunds — Coviello, Gneezy and Goette; competitor poaching — Simonov, Nosko and Rao, read via summaries, S) found up to half of paid traffic lost when the ads stopped and competitors bid on the term. So the haircut is `estimated` anywhere between 0 and 100%, set by the account's own test where one exists and otherwise by competitor presence on the brand term (section 10, step 5), and the packet says which. Size is the dollars per period at risk: a change is sized so that the bear-case loss over the measurement window stays within the owner's loss budget for the account (asked at intake), with the formula printed. A change the loss budget cannot hold is staged — a share of campaigns or a region first — which is also the lift test of section 15.

## 8. The brief

Page one, 350 words or fewer: the plan's row, the thesis in one line, the account reference, the change with its size and start date, bear/base/bull on the primary row at 4 and 13 weeks, wrong-if, and the four exits — the kill level with its two-week rule, the learning-end date before which nothing is judged, the read date on which the rows resolve, and the reversal step. Then the Thesis card and the fixed lines in the order `level-6.md` gives.

## 9. Domain hard rules

Nothing in the account is changed by the pass: no budget, bid, keyword, status or setting is written, and the packet is a change request for the owner or for the account's own change routine with its own approval. Platform connectors are read-only. Every figure carries its as-of date and the attribution model and conversion window it was read under; a figure read under a different window is a different quantity, and conversions are never compared across a window or model change. A change is never scored by its platform-reported outcome alone when a brand or returning-customer share is in play; the row names its incrementality source. Nothing read from the platform's recommendations changes a gate, a bar or a standing answer.
