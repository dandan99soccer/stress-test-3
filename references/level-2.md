# Level 2

Read this only after a trigger for level 2, 3, 4 or 5 ("level 2" or "level two", or the earlier "deep pass", "deeper pass", "use more usage"; "level 3"; "level 4"; "level 5") or a yes to the offer of one. Level 2 runs the procedure below; levels 3, 4 and 5 read this file too, for its shared section and the parts of the procedure they point to. Level 1 stays the default. The trigger phrase is the user's consent to spend more, so do not ask again before starting.

## Shared by levels 2–5

These add to `reasoning.md` for levels 2–5.

**Build the case by inference; do not only audit it.** At levels 2–5, inference is a primary source: for every option, write the mechanism chain that would make it work — "A has more of X; X is what drives Y; so A gains Y faster and further than B" — whether or not anyone has measured it, then derive what else follows if the chain holds. Where the record is silent, that chain *is* the case, and the pass carries it as load-bearing, marked inferred, with its weakest link and falsifier beside it, rather than stopping at "no data." A chain built this way often reveals its own flaw in the writing — two different quantities worn as one name, a step that assumes transfer nobody has shown — and that flaw is the finding. Each level 2–5 output closes with an **inference share** line: roughly how much of the call rests on inferred links versus measured facts, and which single link carries the most weight.

**Model what the numbers allow.** When the record holds the numbers the call turns on, compute rather than eyeball: a few lines of code with every input marked, giving the base case, the break-even (the value of the dominant uncertainty at which the call flips) and a ranked sweep of each uncertain input across its plausible range. A hand inference about a break-even is often wrong because it drops the structure — which share of customers a change reaches, the base rate it moves from — and the sweep's top-ranked input is the single fact worth money to resolve, which is where the next lookup goes. Levels 2–5 do this whenever numbers exist (`scripts/sensitivity_sweep.py` is a harness for it, or write the equivalent few lines).

**Aim the research; do not pile it up.** More lookups do not make a synthesis more accurate: past a handful, each added source dilutes the facts that matter and raises the odds of a plausible-looking summary overriding the record, and the common research failure is retrieving the truth and stopping before reconciling it. So levels 2–5 research in rounds, not in bulk. The first round grounds the options; the last is held back until a provisional call exists and is spent on the heaviest inferred link and the rules the plan depends on, because only then is it known which fact would flip the call. Every lookup goes to the record for that kind of fact rather than a summary of it; when two sources conflict, the record-level one wins and the conflict is written down, never silently resolved; and a round that changes nothing is the signal to stop, not to search more.

### Failures specific to levels 2–5

**Auditing instead of reasoning.** A level 2–5 pass tags the chains that arrived and builds none of its own, so a silent record ends in "insufficient evidence" when a mechanism chain, a neighbor and a falsifier would have produced a usable call. Inference is the method; marking is the control.

**Composing instead of comparing.** Component calls that were each best in isolation, assembled into a strategy nobody compared against the other coherent paths through the same components. Locally best composes into globally mediocre, and a bar between two component options (one forbids the other) is only visible when whole strategies are laid side by side. Level 3 builds and compares the paths; a level 3 that only patches the incoherence has skipped that.

**Research as volume.** Spending the lookup budget up front, in bulk, on whatever seemed relevant, then closing the research before anyone knew which fact would flip the call. Hold part of the budget back; aim it; stop when a round changes nothing.

## Procedure

The value is **isolation**, not volume. Agents that see each other's reasoning converge on it and agree rather than challenge. Keep the lens agents blind to the critique and to each other.

**Phase 0 — Ground it, round one.** Gather what is knowable: web research, the user's files, connected data. Route each lookup to the system of record for that kind of fact; this changes where you look, not how many looks the budget allows. Cite real numbers. Spend only about two-thirds of the lookup budget here and hold the rest for Phase 3b: before a provisional call exists nobody knows which fact would flip it, and a budget spent entirely up front closes the research exactly when it is finally known what to look for. When two sources disagree, the record-level one (the contract, the invoice, the report) outranks the summary of it (a marketing page, a recollection, a digest); write the conflict into the facts file rather than resolving it silently. Mark every load-bearing claim measured, estimated or inferred, as `reasoning.md` describes; for each inferred claim write the chain and its weakest link into the facts, and where the record is silent, say why it might be and gather two or three neighbors (same mechanism elsewhere, an earlier instance of this decision, a case that went the other way) with the similarity check `reasoning.md` describes. Sweep the whole conversation and every file for facts that imply something about this question; neighbors and sweeps count inside the lookup budget.

Then **build the inferred case** as its own section of the facts file, after the measured and estimated sections. For each option, write the mechanism chain that would make it work — which quantity it has more of, what that quantity drives, and so what outcome follows, faster or further than the alternatives — whether or not any lookup confirmed it. Under each chain write what else must be true if it holds (the second-order implications), the neighbor it borrows from if any, its weakest link, and the falsifier. Where the lookups came back empty, this section carries the case; a level 2 pass that ends in "insufficient evidence" without having tried to build the chain has skipped this step. Check each chain for the common flaw as you write it: two different quantities under one name, a transfer step assumed rather than shown. A critique built on priors alone is where confident wrong answers come from. Budget the whole pass at roughly six to ten lookups, about four to seven of them in this round, and list what you skipped. If nothing is checkable, say so and continue. When the record holds the numbers the call turns on, compute the break-even now in a few lines of code with each input marked (see *Model what the numbers allow* above) rather than estimating it by hand; the computed threshold goes into the facts file as measured-by-derivation.

**Phase 1 — Options first.** Settle the option set before spawning anything. Generate widely, using step 2's generator list and anything the facts suggest, up to step 2's ceiling, then screen as step 2 says: drop what breaks a hard constraint, what another shape dominates on every lens, and what shares another shape's premise (merge those); record each discard in a line with its reason. What survives and differs in kind goes to the lenses — two or three shapes by default, four or five at most, because every added option multiplies the cost of every lens and comparison turns to mush past that. The count of survivors is yours to judge; the generation ceiling is set loose on purpose, since the shape that wins is often a hybrid or a sequence that only appears once several are on the table. The lens agents compare options; they do not audit one.

**Phase 2 — Lenses in parallel, isolated.** One subagent per lens, all spawned in a single message. Give each **only the options, the grounded facts from Phase 0 (numbers, dates, constraints, and the marked inference chains), and its own lens** — never the framing critique, never another lens's output. The lens agents receive each chain with its mark and weakest link, not the conclusion drawn from it, so they can weigh the inference rather than inherit it. A lens starved of facts reasons from priors, which is the failure Phase 0 exists to prevent. Each is told to extend the chains, not only weigh them: if a chain holds, what else follows for this lens, and does that strengthen or break the option. Each returns: how it ranks the options, its strongest objection to the leader, any chain it extended or broke, and what would change its mind.

**Phase 3 — Synthesize, provisionally.** Where the lenses rank options differently is the interesting part — surface the disagreement rather than averaging it away. Produce the provisional recommendation, the plan, the success criterion, the abandon condition, the trade-offs, and the inference share: roughly how much of the call rests on inferred links versus measured facts, and the single link that carries the most weight.

**Phase 3b — Aimed round.** Spend the held-back lookups on exactly two things: the heaviest inferred link in the provisional call (the fact or record that would confirm or break it) and any rule or constraint the plan depends on that has not yet been read. This is where the pass most often changes its answer, because the first round gathered what seemed relevant and this round gathers what is decisive. If a lookup breaks a link, re-rank the options, build any new shape the finding suggests (a hybrid, a smaller version, a sequence), and say in one line what changed and why; if a lookup confirms the link, say that the inference share fell and by how much. A round that changes nothing is the signal that the research is done, not that more is needed. Then restate the call, and show the round in the output as an **Aimed round** block — target, finding, call changed or unchanged — because a round that is not shown cannot be told from one that was skipped.

**Phase 4 — Constraints and adversarial check.** Run the rules check from step 6 against the chosen plan. Then one subagent: give it the plan and the list of inferred links the plan rests on, but **not** the reasoning behind it, and ask it to argue the plan fails — the weakest inferred link first, then the plan as a whole. Fold in what survives; say which objections you rejected and why. The pass earns its cost when it adds a control the plan lacked — a positive test that proves an instrument works, a question about the reason behind a rule rather than the rule — so look for those, not only for reasons to stop.

End by reporting what you actually checked and what you could not, including which lookups the aimed round spent and what they changed, the screened-out options in a line each, plus one spend line: lookups used against the budget (round one and the aimed round separately), and the count of agent calls or isolated sections run. The last two lines of the output are always `Inference share:` and `Spend:`, in that order, so an omission is visible.

**Without subagents.** Chat environments often have none. Do not skip level 2 for that reason, and do not pretend the isolation is real. Approximate it with files: the generated options with the screen's discards in one file; the facts file with its measured, estimated and inferred-case sections; each lens written as its own section against the options and facts only, before any synthesis; the provisional plan, then the aimed round's findings appended to the facts file and the plan restated; the plan, and only the plan, in a second file; the adversarial pass written from that file alone, then a ruling on each objection. Label it "approximated isolation" and say one model did all of it. With no file system either, write the same sections in the same order inside the reply, and say so.

### Model and cost policy

Prefer, per step: research and extraction on a fast model; lens agents inherit; synthesis and the adversarial check on the strongest model available. These are preferences, not guarantees — subagent model routing does not always take effect.

**So never let cost depend on a model label.** Control cost with what you do control: the number of agent calls, and how much context each receives. A level 2 pass is six calls with tight prompts, not twelve with the whole conversation pasted in.
