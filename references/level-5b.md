# Level 5 procedure, part two — models, adjudication, the re-open round

Read this with `level-5.md`, before the intake: the model rules apply from the first message, and Phases 5b and 5c run between Phase 5 and Phase 6.

## Models and effort

Level 5 spends its strongest reasoning where the logic is hardest and its cheapest where the work is retrieval. The session's model and effort are the ceiling: nothing the pass spawns runs above either, except Fable where this section names it. Within that ceiling each role has a fixed model and a fixed effort, both named on the call; the lead does not choose case by case, and the pass scales down only where a step does not need the lead's judgment — retrieval, never grading — because a high effort on a step that does not need it spends usage like any other waste. Every level file names its own values; this section is the shared rule.

- **Lead — Opus, at xhigh for levels 5 and 6** (high at levels 1–4). The plan, the final grades, every Phase 3 inference, the disconfirmation verdict, the brief and the packet run in the main context, so they run on the session's model at the session's effort. The intake states both in a `Model:` line above the pace line — model and effort, what the agents will be called with, and the adjudication rule in a few words; where the surface does not show the session's effort, the line says `effort: not shown` and the calls below use the level's named values. On Sonnet or Haiku, the line adds that the judgment will run on that model and that switching to Opus before replying gives a stronger call; if the owner does not switch, the pass continues, every agent is capped at that model, and the Gate line adds `lead Opus or above ✗`. Below the level's named effort, the same: the line says so, every agent is capped at the session's effort, and the Gate line adds `effort <the level's> or above ✗`. On Fable, the line adds that the lead's own steps cost about two and a half times Opus at API rates, that the spawned pieces still run on Opus at their level's effort, and that an Opus lead with the Fable adjudication below gets most of the gain for much less.
- **Analysis of research never runs on a research agent's model or effort.** Grading, reconciling, inference and the disconfirmation verdict run on the lead, Opus at minimum, at the lead's effort. Where `references/owner-profile.md` allows it, Fable may take an analysis step the lead judges complex or vital, at the lead's effort, logged in `FABLE-LOG.md` with the reason; absent that line, or the file, Fable runs only as the adjudicator below.
- **Replications, and level 4's isolated pieces — Opus at high, both named on the call.** The two replications and, when level 4 runs inside this level, its Phase 3 assessments, Phase 4 derivations and Phase 5 red-team passes are spawned with the model and the effort passed explicitly (`opus`, `high`): each is a level 4 through first synthesis and runs at level 4's named values, so a default set elsewhere cannot quietly downgrade them, and an xhigh lead does not pay xhigh for pieces whose level names high. Under an Opus lead that is the lead's own model, for the reason `level-5.md` Phase 5 gives. Under a Fable lead they stay on Opus — a Fable replication costs about what a whole Opus level 4 costs, and when agreement is short of full the adjudication below settles it by chain, not by count — and the Agreement line adds `replications: Opus under a Fable lead`. Under a lead below Opus or below high they are capped at the lead's.
- **Research agents — Sonnet, one effort step below the lead and never below medium,** as `research.md` section 7 sets out: targeted retrieval and extraction at that step; route searches, stance research and the disconfirmation lookups at high, capped at the lead's effort, because a route search's value is its persistence. Never stronger than the lead, never below Sonnet — a Sonnet lead's agents stay on Sonnet — and never Haiku, except a scripted data pull the lead reads itself (`research-6b.md` section 1).
- **Adjudicator — Fable at the lead's effort, when the logic is tricky** (Phase 5b). The one place a model stronger than the lead runs: it reads little and decides the call, so the stronger model costs little there; its effort is the session's, never above it.

Pass the model and the effort on every agent call (in Claude Code, the Agent tool's `model` and `effort` fields; the same two in a Workflow script). Where the environment ignores either — a surface without the field, a session-wide effort setting that overrides calls — the agent inherits the lead's and the Spend line says so; the Spend line counts agents by model and effort (`agents: 7 opus/high, 4 sonnet/medium, 1 fable/xhigh`). Without subagents (claude.ai chat) everything runs on the model and effort chosen in the picker: choose Opus or Fable, at high or above, before triggering level 5, and the Spend line names what ran.

## Phase 5b — Adjudication

Phase 5's rule stands — the call is chosen by chain against the verified ledger, never by count — and this phase decides who applies it. **The logic is tricky** when any of these hold: agreement is short of full (2 of 3, no two agree, 1 of 2); the call changed during Phases 2–5; or the single link carrying the most weight is inferred rather than measured. Then one fresh agent on **Fable** (`fable` named on the call) adjudicates. Otherwise the lead does, and the line reads `Adjudication: lead — 3 of 3, call unchanged, heaviest link measured`.

The adjudicator is blind like the replications. It gets the decision as framed, the objectives, the verified ledger with its marks and grades, the pre-registered flip conditions, the brief's question table and disconfirmation section, the inferred claims with premise, weakest link and falsifier, and the competing analyses — the original and each replication, as call, fallback and heaviest links — labeled A, B and C in shuffled order, without saying which is the lead's. Not the provisional call, the packet or the discussion. It is asked to check every step of each chain against the ledger and flag each that fails — a mark its source does not support, two quantities under one name, a transfer assumed rather than shown, a base rate applied without its similarity check; to rule which analysis's chain survives, or that none does, which is "decide nothing yet" with the fact named; to check the disconfirmation verdict against the pre-registered conditions; and to name the one fact that would most change its ruling. It returns rulings, one line each, not an essay.

The lead adopts the ruling unless it can name the ledger entry the adjudicator misread; an overrule goes into the packet with that entry. The ruling goes in the Agreement block as `Adjudication: Fable — <ruling>`, and each flagged step is fixed in the ledger or answered in the brief. Without subagents the adjudication cannot be blind: the lead applies Phase 5's rule and writes `Adjudication: lead (no subagents)`.

## Phase 5c — Re-open round

The research closes at Phase 4, but Phases 3–5b can surface what it never looked at. Run one more aimed round when any of these fired: the call changed during Phases 2–5b — the new leader has had no search for the evidence against it; the replications split on a fact a lookup could settle; the adjudicator's deciding fact is one a lookup could settle; or Phase 3, the sweep or the red team raised a question whose answer would cross a flip threshold. Write each new question's flip threshold before searching. Up to about six lookups, dispatched as `research.md` section 7 says; when the call changed, three or four of them are a disconfirmation round on the new leader, in their own agent, judged against flip conditions written before it runs. Apply what comes back by its pre-registered thresholds, update the ledger, the model and the brief, and say in one line what moved. One round only: what it leaves open goes to the Cheapest test, the Kill criteria or Phase 6, never to a second round or a second adjudication. When nothing fired, write `Re-open round: not triggered` and go on. The fast form runs it only when the call changed, at three or four lookups.

## Research additions borrowed from level 6

A level 5 reads `research-6.md` and `research-6b.md` with `research.md` and takes this subset, sized to fit one sitting and research.md's lookup budget: the eight added routes whenever a round comes back empty; the evidence rules of research-6b section 2 for every agent (neutral framing, the targeted trigger, verbatim capture before summary, tables through code, no self-review or debate, the return form of section 3 with no grades from agents); the evidence map with its `Coverage:`, `Traced:` and `Sources reached:` lines in the brief header, counted against the routes until a pack exists; paired stance agents on Q1 only; the missed-question agent once, before Phase 1, blind to the plan; a disconfirmation agent per strategy in contention, none told which leads (one agent on the top two in shuffled order when usage is tight); `cite_check.py` over the sources table before the packet, and the Opus support check on the load-bearing citations only. No extra rounds: research.md section 6 and Phase 5c still govern. The fast form takes only the routes, the three header lines and `cite_check.py`.

## Simulation borrowed from level 6

When the test in `references/level-6-simulation.md` section 1 holds — the decision is linked to things that move each other, the call could flip inside a link's range, and the main links can be sized from data in reach — a level 5 runs the cause-and-effect simulation once, in Phase 1 beside the research plan, under the same usage gate: the small case (a graph of up to about eight nodes fitted from a panel already in reach, about two to four points) runs without asking; a run above about ten points or needing agents to size links is asked about once, in one message with the estimate and what it would decide; unattended it is listed as due. Its sensitivity table adds the links that cross the flip to the plan as questions with the data route first; its rows go in `FORECASTS.csv` with the others; the packet carries the `Simulation:` line under `Re-open round:`. The fast form runs it only when the panel is already in reach, and never a second graph.

## Forecast ledger

Phase 7's ranged probabilities, the named-case base rates they rest on, and the robustness table's futures when the pass puts numbers on them are written as rows of `FORECASTS.csv` in the format of `references/forecast.md`: each with its resolution criterion, source and date, its range, the forecaster and method, the base rate with its class and n, the running model's cutoff, and one line each on why it might be too high and too low. Before the packet is delivered, `scripts/forecast_ledger.py --check` passes and `--append` has added the rows to the shared record — the folder `forecast.md` section 9 resolves, which `--where` prints, or the connected sheet in chat. The packet opens its probabilities block with a `Calibration:` line read from `CALIBRATION.md` there (`n/a (n=<count>)` under fifteen resolved rows) and naming where the record lives, and the refresh plan names the dates on which `--score` and `--calibrate` run — the review-date task of `forecast.md` section 13 is what runs them. The owner's own numbers, when given, are rows with forecaster `owner`, elicited before Claude's are shown. Two rules from the first point-in-time run (18 Oct 2026): every target a rule in force or a proposed rule names is a pre-registered option, whether or not the lead favors it; and when a default or a rule fixes the direction of the call, the packet says "direction fixed by constraints; the research and forecasts bear on size and timing" in its first line after the call, instead of presenting a constrained call as a researched one.
