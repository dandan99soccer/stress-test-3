# Research at level 6, part one — new routes, creativity, completeness

The cause-and-effect simulation of `level-6-simulation.md` (owner requirement, 18 Oct 2026) is part of the prior model: its sensitivity table names research questions, each with the data route first.

Read this with `research.md` (whose ten routes, grading, inference moves and brief template stand) before a level 6 research plan. Level 5 borrows the subset that `level-5b.md` names; a level 5 does not run everything here. Level 5 researches once, by routes that read; level 6 researches in rounds aimed at the questions that still straddle their flip thresholds, by routes that also compute, replay and price, and it measures how much of the evidence space it touched instead of asserting completeness. It is built against two measured failures of the real level 5 runs: the tail risk that moved the call surfaced only in a re-open round, because the research followed the ledger's existing beliefs; and empty routes were accepted after one phrasing while two load-bearing documents returned errors and were left unread. Part two (`research-6b.md`) holds dispatch, the evidence rules and the agent forms.

## 1. Eight routes added to section 2 of research.md

- **Data.** Pull the series and compute the statistic in code, with the as-of date on every input and a look-ahead guard: base rates from a price panel, revision trends, drawdown frequencies, cohort outcomes, filing lags. The lead or an Opus agent writes or reviews the computation; a Haiku agent may run it and return the numbers with the script path. Mark `measured (computed)`. Tables and datasets go through code, never through the prompt.
- **Experiment and replay.** When the method itself is uncertain, run it on past dates with sealed truth (a point-in-time replay) inside the session. A live test that touches anyone or any money goes into the Cheapest test block for the owner to run, its threshold written before it runs; the pass never schedules it, and its result comes back as a tripwire like a Phase 6 reply.
- **Market-implied.** Options-implied distributions and event moves, credit and deal spreads, prediction-market prices, futures-implied paths. The best available forecast often exists already and must be found before the pass claims to know better; it is read with its known biases (a risk-neutral probability of a good-state event is a lower bound; a thin market's longshot price is an upper bound).
- **Crowd.** Forecasting platforms, consensus estimates with their dispersion and known lean, professional surveys.
- **Incentive and insider.** Who profits from each belief; the filings and actions those parties take with their own money; each question routed to the source least exposed to the incentive.
- **Adjacent party.** Suppliers, customers, competitors, regulators and counterparties, whose filings, calls and dockets mention the subject without the subject's framing.
- **Negative space.** For each signal that should be present if a belief holds, check whether it is. Absence of expected evidence is graded evidence: no insider buying after a large fall; no customer announcement behind a claimed win; no filing behind an alleged dispute.
- **Docket and litigation.** Court records, regulatory proceedings, freedom-of-information releases, procurement records.

The direct route now means the connected system of record first (an account, a CRM, analytics, a filing database) and the web second; the brief's header carries `Sources reached:` naming the systems read directly.

## 2. Creativity: finding what the ledger does not already believe

Each step has an artifact that reaches the plan; a step with no artifact did not run.

1. **Backward from outcomes.** For each leaf of the outcome space: if this outcome is coming, what evidence would already exist today? Each answer is a plan question with a flip threshold. This is the step that would have found the tail risk in round one, and it produces the signposts for free.
2. **Hypotheses before search.** List the rival explanations of the known facts (a price is down: multiple compression, an earnings cut, a forced seller, fraud risk, a sector rotation) and the observation that discriminates each pair; the discriminators are plan questions, the first explanation that arrived is not.
3. **Paired stance researchers** on the top questions by value: two agents with opposed briefs (the short seller and the long; the vendor's champion and the former customer; the historian and the practitioner), each told to find what the other would miss, never shown the other's return and never asked to debate. Artifact: a **stance diff**, one row per claim found by one stance and not the other, each becoming a graded finding or a next-round question with its threshold.
4. **Analogy with a quota.** Six candidate analogues from at least three domains or eras, each scored on the four or five structural dimensions that drive the outcome (scale, incentives, reversibility, time, who bears the cost); the top two are researched as named-case questions. The count is what produces the non-obvious reference class.
5. **People first.** The three to five people or institutions who would know (the former employee, the regulator, the supplier's finance chief, the author of the key paper, the critic who published), what each has said on it, and the question each would be asked in the elicitation phase.
6. **Incentive mapping.** Who wants each belief held and what they would suppress; the suppressed thing is a negative-space question.
7. **Temporal deep-dive**, mandatory for the top question: its history over five to ten years (longer when the class has a long forecast record), what was forecast at each juncture and what happened; the forecast track record of the class is the base rate for the pass's own forecast.
8. **Reframing round.** Three alternative framings of the decision and the one research question each would add; kept when its answer would change the call under the main framing too.
9. **The missed-question agent.** A blind agent on Opus (Fable only where the owner's rules already allow it) receives the decision in neutral form and the outcome space without probabilities, never the plan, the call, the fallback, the disconfirmation verdict or what changed, and returns the questions it would ask and the evidence types it would read. It runs before round one and, at level 6, again after the last round, when it also receives the question table with the status column removed and the sources table. Artifact: each unmatched question added to the plan with a threshold, or closed with a written reason.
10. **Research pre-mortem.** "It is a year on and the call was wrong because the research missed X": five X's written before the packet, each checked or named as open in the brief.

## 3. Completeness, measured

1. **Evidence map.** The evidence-type checklist crossed with the questions: each cell touched or untouched, what was found, its grade, and the reason an untouched type was skipped. The checklist is the pack's when one exists (`references/packs/<pack>.md`); until then it is the eighteen routes (research.md section 2 and section 1 here), and the Coverage line says `checklist: routes`. For the top three questions by value every applicable type is touched or a reason is written.
2. **Traceability.** Every load-bearing number is traced to a primary document actually read, counted as `Traced: n of m`. An untraceable load-bearing claim gets a dedicated fetch by two more routes (the DOI or accession number, the author's own page, an archive copy) before it may stay untraceable; a document that returns an error is pulled raw or from an archive before the route is called empty.
3. **Two readers.** Any table, figure or series that crosses a flip threshold or enters the model is read by two independent readers, one of whom is always the lead or an Opus agent (the other may be a Haiku or Sonnet extractor), and the numbers must match before entry; a disagreement is `unresolved`, never averaged.
4. **Party, horizon and strategy completeness.** Every stakeholder has had its angle looked at; every horizon in the outcome space has at least one evidence item; every strategy still in contention gets its own disconfirmation agent, given the strategy under test and its mechanism and never told which strategy leads (when usage allows one agent, it takes the top two in shuffled order). This supersedes research.md section 7's sentence that the disconfirmation agent gets the leading strategy.
5. **Empty routes.** An empty route lists the phrasings, places and dates tried, and for a top-three question is retried once by a different stance agent before it counts as empty. A search cap or tool error is recorded as `not searched`, never as empty: an empty search of the right places is evidence, an empty search of the wrong places is not, and a search that never ran is neither.
6. **Stop by value.** A question closes when its bounded range (from bounds, or `scripts/sensitivity_sweep.py`) no longer crosses its flip threshold, or when two independent routes agree within the threshold's precision. A further round is worth running only while an open question still straddles its threshold. At level 5, research.md section 6 and Phase 5c govern how many rounds there are; at level 6, `level-6.md` caps the loop at three. The disconfirmation share of a round is never dropped.
7. **Surprise ledger.** Every surprise becomes a question in the next round or a line saying why it changes nothing. The IBKR run left five surprises in a list; one of them should have been round two.
8. **Sources reached.** Named in the brief header, connected systems against the web.

## 4. The three lines, the map and the sources table

The brief's header carries three lines a reader can check without reading the brief: `Coverage: <touched> of <applicable> evidence types for Q1–Q3 (checklist: routes | <pack>); untouched: <type (reason)>, ...`; `Traced: <n> of <m> load-bearing claims to a primary read; secondary: <ids>`; `Sources reached: <systems read directly>; web for the rest`. The evidence map follows the question table as `| Q | evidence type | touched | found | grade | reason if untouched |`, one row per applicable type. The sources table of research.md section 5 gains three columns, `Link`, `Access date` and `Provenance` (verified passage, primary read, or secondary), because `scripts/cite_check.py` reads the links and the second reader reads the provenance. The packet quotes the three header lines under its own `Coverage:` line (a level 6 fixed line; at level 5 they stay in the brief header). A brief whose coverage line names no untouched type has not used the checklist: every real decision leaves types untouched, and the point is to say which and why.

## 5. What this part does not change

Grades (replication, sample match, independence), the inference moves of research.md section 4, the brief template of section 5 and the retrieval-only rule for agents all stand. The research plan still lists questions, not sources, with a flip threshold written before any search; it now also lists, per question, the evidence types applicable, the stance pairing if any, the round in which the question is first researched, its lookup budget, and the creativity step that generated it.
