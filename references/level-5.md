# Level 5 procedure — research, validate, replicate

Read this only after the user has said "level 5" (or "level five") about a decision. An explicit request runs this level; when the gate in SKILL.md fails, open with the one-line warning and continue into the intake. Level 5 runs on top of a level 4: it needs level 4's ledger, table, objectives, model and packet, so when none exists it runs level 4 first (reading `intake.md`, `level-4.md` and `level-4b.md`) and then continues here; the intake runs once, at the start, with this level's question count. Read `research.md` before Phase 1 and `level-5b.md` before the intake. Level 4 remains the right tool when its packet's weakest element is anything but information; this level earns its cost when the call still rests on claims whose provenance is thin or on questions nobody has researched, when a wrong call would be severe and hard to undo, and when the decision is not due before the research can land.

**Pace.** A level 5 finishes in one sitting. Nothing in it waits on anyone but the owner's intake reply (or, in budget mode, the usage reset): the replications run while the research does, research questions run in parallel where `research.md` section 7 says they should, and the people asked in Phase 6 are not waited for — their replies are tripwires that update the packet when they land. The **fast** form runs on "level 5 fast", "quick level 5", a time limit given with the trigger ("level 5 in 20 minutes"), "fast" in reply to the intake, or a deadline tighter than the standard pass: it keeps every phase but researches only the top four to six questions, runs the disconfirmation round at four to six lookups, builds named-case tables for the leader and the runner-up only, runs one replication, and caps the packet at about 1,200 words; the questions it did not research go under **Answers that would change this** with their thresholds. Where a clock can be read and a time limit was given, note the start and check it at each phase boundary; once about three-quarters of the time is gone, close every open question at its current status — answered, bounded, inferred or open — and go to Phase 4. Open with `Mode: level 5 fast (triggered by "<phrase>")` when it runs.

**What this level adds.** Level 4 researches in aimed rounds and marks every claim for how it is known. Level 5 does not add volume to that. It adds seven things level 4 cannot do inside one run: it asks the owner what only the owner knows before it spends; it plans the research as questions ranked by what they could change and runs each by several routes, including the ones that look for the evidence against; it grades every load-bearing claim at its primary source; it infers what no source shows — by analogy, bounds and chains — marked so it can be attacked; it builds the base rate from named cases with outcomes; it replicates the whole analysis twice in fresh, blind contexts and reports how many runs agree; and it compiles all of this into one brief, organized by question, that a reader can check — then brings the questions that remain to the people who can answer them.

**Phase 0 — Intake, scope, pre-registration.** Run the intake (`intake.md`): up to eight questions (three on the fast form), closing with the short list of documents and data the owner can supply and the pace line; whatever arrives is read in Phase 2 as a primary source. As soon as the intake closes, spawn the Phase 5 replications — they need nothing the research produces — and carry on while they run. Then, from the level 4 packet, list the load-bearing claims and critical uncertainties: every measured or estimated fact and every inferred chain whose change would move the call, the fallback or a threshold — usually six to twelve — each with what would change if it failed, which is the value of verifying it; claims the call does not depend on are not researched at this level. Pre-register, before any search, the finding that would flip the call to the runner-up and the finding that would send the decision back to "decide nothing yet". Pre-registration guards against reading whatever turns up as consistent with the call.

**Phase 1 — Research plan.** Write the plan as `research.md` section 1 describes: one question per load-bearing claim and critical uncertainty, its flip threshold, its routes — at least two for anything that could flip the call, one of them not direct — and its budget, ranked by value of information. Close in a line any question whose answer could change nothing. Say which questions the intake answers already closed. End the plan with its **Dispatch** line per question — in-line, or handed to a research agent and on which model — as `research.md` section 7 sets out.

**Phase 2 — Gather by route and grade.** Run the plan in ranked order, by the routes in `research.md` section 2: spawn the dispatched questions' agents together in one message, work the in-line questions while they run, then grade what they return as section 7 says. Three pieces are mandatory for every strategy still in contention. The **provenance audit**: the primary source of each load-bearing claim, read — the contract rather than the summary of it, the original study rather than the article that cited it, the system of record rather than the export — and graded on replication, sample match and independence, with `untraceable` where no primary source can be reached and one line saying how much of the call rests on untraceable claims. The **disconfirmation round**: six to ten lookups whose only target is evidence that the leading strategy fails or that its mechanism does not hold, routed to the kinds of source most likely to hold a counter-case, then judged against the pre-registered conditions — stands, flips, or decide nothing yet with the fact named — and reported with where it looked, because an empty search of the right places is evidence and an empty search of the wrong places is not. The **named-case reference class**: six or more cases per strategy where the record allows, each with the outcome on the objective that matters, the source, and a one-line similarity check; the base rate computed from the table and set against the figure level 4 used — where they differ, the table governs; with fewer than a handful of cases the figure stays marked estimated and this case's uniqueness is named as the weakest link, not as a reason the base rate does not apply. A number that changes on verification is re-entered in the model or the robustness table and level 4's outputs re-run; record what moved.

**Phase 3 — Infer what the record cannot show.** For every question still open after Phase 2, use the moves in `research.md` section 4 — structural analogy with its similarity check, bounding, mechanism chains with falsifiers, backward reasoning from the call, consilience across routes — and write each result as a marked claim with its premise, weakest link and falsifier. Then sweep: connect every fact in hand, the intake answers and the lookups made for other questions included, and write the implications nobody asked for. Inference here is the method, not a confession; a level 5 that ends a question at "no data" when a neighbor, a bound and a chain were available has skipped this phase. Each inferred claim is swept in the model like any estimate.

**Phase 4 — Compile the brief.** Write the research brief, by question, in the template of `research.md` section 5: the findings with their grades, the conflicts and how each was resolved, the inferred claims with their marks, what each question does to the strategies, the confidence and why, what remains open with its cheapest closer, the reference-class tables, the disconfirmation verdict with where it looked, the surprises, what changed in the model, and the sources. Update the ledger so its marks and grades agree with the brief. Then restate the provisional call and say what the research changed: the call, the fallback, a threshold, a probability, or nothing.

**Phase 5 — Independent replications.** Two fresh contexts — subagents that have seen nothing of this conversation — each receive only the decision as stated, the owner, the time available, the intake answers, and the raw record (the documents and data; not the ledger, not the table, not the objectives, not the brief, not the call), and are asked to run level 4 through its first synthesis and return: objectives, strategy table, call and fallback, and the three heaviest links. Spawn both in one message, when Phase 0 closes, so neither can see the other and both run while the research does; they run on the lead's own model, named on the call, because a disagreement from a weaker model cannot be told from a gap in capability. The fast form runs one, and reports **2 of 2** or **1 of 2**. Compare the three analyses — the original and the two replications — on four things: did they name the same objectives, generate the same strategies, reach the same call, and rest on the same links? Report the agreement first, as a count: **3 of 3**, **2 of 3**, or **no two agree**. Agreement by different routes is strong; agreement by the same route is weaker, and say which. Anything short of full agreement means the call is close or contested, and the packet says so in those words: the stated confidence is capped, the disagreement leads the packet, and the fact that would settle it is named. The recommendation is then chosen by chain against the verified ledger — never by count, never by preferring the longer run — but it is labeled provisional, and the dissenting run's call enters the packet as the fallback or as a decision point with its trigger. Every other divergence is a finding too: an objective one run named and another omitted is a frame error to resolve with the owner; a strategy one run generated and another missed goes onto the table and through level 4's assessment; when all agree on the call but differ on the first step, the step that survives is the one every run's premises permit — usually the question that selects between them, asked before either step is taken. Without subagents this phase cannot be done honestly — one model re-deriving from its own context is not a replication — so say so, skip it, and report the spend saved; do not fake it with a file section. A replication that runs inside a subagent uses file-isolated sections for its own derivations and says so. Then run Phases 5b and 5c in `level-5b.md`: the adjudication, on Fable when the logic is tricky, and one re-open round when a late phase found what the research missed.

**Phase 6 — Elicitation.** For each uncertainty still critical after Phases 1–5, name the person whose answer would move it — the stakeholder who disagrees, the expert with the reference class in their head, the vendor or counterparty — and write the question for each, with the answer threshold stated before it is asked, so the reply can be read without rationalizing. Where a connected tool can deliver the question and the user says yes, send it and set the reply as a tripwire; otherwise the questions go in the brief. Do not hold the packet for replies. When one lands later, read it against its written threshold before any discussion, record it in the ledger with its own mark, and re-issue only the packet blocks it changes — the call, the fallback, a threshold or a probability — saying what moved; that is an update to this level 5, not a new one.

**Phase 7 — Validated packet.** Re-score the six elements; the information score should have moved, and the pass says by how much and why. Deliver level 4's packet with these blocks, in this order straight after the call:

- **Agreement:** N of 3 runs (N of 2 on the fast form), by which routes, and the words "close call" or "contested" when it is short of full agreement; then the `Adjudication:` and `Re-open round:` lines.
- **Cheapest test:** the single unknown that would most change the call and the cheapest way to resolve it — a small test, a person, a document — with its cost, its time, and the threshold at which the call changes.
- **Kill criteria:** what would prove the call wrong (an observable with a number), the signal to watch and where it is read, who checks it and by when, and what happens when it fires. These replace the abandon line; the success line stays.
- **Answers that would change this:** every intake question left unanswered, with the value at which the call changes.

Then the validation appendix: the research brief, or its path, with the provenance table, the disconfirmation verdict against the pre-registered conditions, the named-case base rates against the figures they replaced, the replication comparison on its four points with every divergence and how it was resolved, the open questions with their thresholds and who holds them, and the refresh plan — the review date on which the disconfirmation search is re-run, the signposts re-read, the recorded probabilities scored and the decision re-opened on purpose. The probabilities in the decision record carry a range, and any that departs from the named-case base rate carries its written reason. The decision record file gains an **Outcome** section, left blank, to be filled at the review with what happened and a score: a decision judged only by its outcome teaches nothing, and one never scored teaches less. Restate the call and say in one line what the whole level changed: the call, the fallback, a threshold, a probability, or nothing. Make the usual filing offer, the review date included.

### Cost policy

Expensive by design and bounded by value: only load-bearing claims are researched; the disconfirmation round is one round; the replications are two (one on the fast form); elicitation is one question per critical uncertainty. Stop a question when two routes agree or two rounds change nothing. Typical spend for the spend line: eight to sixteen lookups across the plan, six to twelve primary-source reads, the named-case tables, two replication runs (each a level 4 through first synthesis; together the largest item at this level), and the questions sent; the Spend line counts agents by model. Handing research to agents on a lighter model costs less of the plan's usage than the lead reading every page, when a question needs several lookups. When time is tight, run the fast form and say so; tight usage follows `budget.md`; never drop the disconfirmation round, because a level 5 without the search for the evidence against is only a longer level 4. If primary sources cannot be reached — a contract the user cannot share, a study behind a wall — the claim stays at its level 4 mark with "primary unavailable" beside it, and the pass continues. Where the decision is internal and every source is already primary (an invoice, a clause), the provenance audit is short and the weight falls on the disconfirmation round, Phase 3 and Phase 5; say so rather than padding. The packet stays under about 1,800 words plus the delivered files.
