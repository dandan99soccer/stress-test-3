# Level 4 procedure, part two — judge and ship

Read this after `level-4.md`. It carries Phases 3–6 and the cost policy; the standard, the frame, the objectives, the regime line, the table, the ledger, the model and the aimed rounds are in part one.

**Phase 3 — Score every strategy in isolation.** One assessment per surviving strategy, each seeing the table, the bars, the objectives, the ledger and the model's outputs — never another strategy's assessment, never a derivation. Each scores its strategy against every objective in turn, from evidence in the ledger with its marks, before forming any overall view; then applies the two always-on lenses and the two profile lenses that bite hardest; then returns its ranking of all the strategies from its own seat, the strongest objection to its own strategy, the single fact that would flip its ranking, and any dealbreaker — a hard constraint the strategy breaks, which ends its evaluation early rather than being averaged away. With subagents, one per strategy, spawned in a single message; without them, one file per strategy, written before any derivation is read.

**Phase 4 — Three blind derivations by assigned routes.** Three independent answers to the top-level question from the ledger, the table, the objectives and the model only, blind to Phase 3 and to each other, each by a different route so that agreement means something: one from the outside view (reference classes and base rates first, specifics second); one from the inside view (the mechanism chains and the model's break-evens); one from robustness (which strategy fails in the fewest futures, and the story of how the leader fails). A fresh subagent each, or three file-isolated sections written straight after the ledger and model and before any assessment is read. Then adjudicate — never by count. Independent reasoners who see each other's answers converge on the majority, and when correct answers are generated by one route and discarded by the vote the loss is large; so check each derivation's chain against the ledger, name the weakest link in each, and the call goes to the chain that survives, even when it is the minority. Three routes reaching one call by different chains is the strongest signal this level produces; say which happened. Routes that agree on the call but not on the fallback have found something: the fallback goes into the packet as a decision point with its trigger and each branch's case, not as a footnote. Then the first synthesis: the derivations' call, read against the Phase 3 profile; the strategy chosen; a final holistic judgment, allowed only now that the profile is complete and said in one line if it moves the call; and the master plan with owners and dates where they exist.

**Phase 5 — Dialectical red team.** Five passes, each isolated from the reasoning that produced the plan:

- *Attack.* A fresh context receives the chosen plan, the ledger with its marks and the inferred links the plan rests on — not the reasoning, not the derivations, not the assessments — and argues as hard as it can that the plan fails: the weakest inferred link first, then the mechanism, then the plan as a whole. Rule on each point; only what survives counts, and every survivor becomes a control, a tripwire or a change to the plan. Attacking a plan tends to harden it, which is why this pass never stands alone: the counter-plan tests whether the plan should win at all.
- *Counter-plan.* Build the strongest case **for** the best runner-up from the same ledger and objectives, as a plan someone would actually run. The winner must beat it on the objectives, not merely survive objections to itself; arguing only against a plan tends to harden it. If the runner-up wins the comparison, say so and revisit.
- *Pre-mortem.* It is the review date and the chosen strategy has failed; write the story, concrete causes in order. Imagining the outcome as certain produces more and more specific causes than asking for risks. Each cause becomes a tripwire or a control in the plan, or is ruled out with a reason.
- *Real dissent.* Where the record names someone who disagrees — a colleague, a customer, the finance owner — argue from their actual interests first; and ask the user whether anyone genuinely disagrees, because an authentic dissenter outperforms any assigned one. Their strongest case is summarized before it is answered.
- *Bias checks.* Would this strategy be chosen starting fresh today, with nothing already spent? Which number in the record set the ranges, and does the call survive a different anchor? Which choice would be regretted more at the review date — and if the honest answer favours change, is caution the real bias? For a transformative choice, whose preferences cannot be forecast from today's, favour the staged strategy.

Rule on every objection and keep what survives; the pass earns its cost when it adds a control the plan lacked.

**Phase 6 — Decision record and packet.** Score the six elements again and name the weakest; it is the one line the owner most needs to read. Then the packet, under about 1,500 words plus the delivered files — a limit on the reply, kept by moving detail into the files: the Mode and Gate lines; the frame, the owner, the objectives with their weights and constraints; the five-part regime line in full (reversibility, time, feedback validity, recurrence, defensible probabilities); the strategy table laid out in full — every strategy on one line with what it is, what it is best at, its base case against the top objectives or the futures that break it, and its reference-class prior with the named class and base rate, or "none found" — with the discards and bars beneath; the common-ground moves; the call and the master plan; straight after them the **Cheapest test** block — the single unknown that would most change the call and the cheapest way to resolve it, with cost, time and the threshold at which the call changes — and the **Kill criteria** block — what would prove the call wrong as an observable with a number, the signal to watch and where it is read, who checks it and by when, and what happens when it fires; **Answers that would change this** — every intake question left unanswered, with the value at which the call changes; the three derivations by route and how any dissent was resolved; the attack's surviving points, the counter-plan verdict and the red-team rulings; the success line with its threshold from the model (the kill criteria replace the abandon line); a monitoring plan of two or three tripwires, each an observable with a number, a date and a named checker, with the signposts from the robustness table where one was built; a review date on which the decision is re-opened on purpose; the probabilities — that the success condition is met by its date, and that each of the one or two load-bearing assumptions holds — stated as numbers so they can be scored; the scorecard; what was checked against what was skipped; and, as the last two lines, `Inference share:` and `Spend:` — lookups per round, model runs, and the count of agent calls or isolated sections. Then commitment: who must act, what each must commit to, the known dissenter and how their concern is answered, and for a group decision the one-line protocol — individual estimates in writing before the meeting, minority view first, a named owner, disagree-and-commit once decided. Deliver the ledger, the model and a **decision record** as files: the record holds the options, the call, the probabilities, the success condition, the kill criteria, the review date and a blank **Outcome** section, so that at the review what happened and a score can be written beside them; a decision judged only by its outcome teaches nothing. The probabilities also go into a `FORECASTS.csv` beside the decision record, one row each with its resolution criterion, source and date, range, forecaster, method and base rate, in the format of `references/forecast.md`; run `scripts/forecast_ledger.py --check` on it, append it to the shared record with `--append`, and at the review resolve and score the rows from their named sources, filling the Outcome section from the ledger. Make the usual filing offer. Where a licensed professional or a named owner makes the call, the packet is their brief, with the critical uncertainties written as the questions to bring.

### Cost policy

Spend at this level follows the weakest element, not a fixed count, and it is bounded by value rather than by a cap: a lookup, a test or a question to the owner is worth running when the swing it could cause exceeds the delay it costs, and not otherwise. The counts in the phases — eight to twelve lookups, then up to two rounds of six to eight, up to about eight strategies, three derivations, four red-team passes — describe a typical run so the spend line can be read, not a target to reach. Stop early whenever a round changes nothing or the weakest element is already at the point where more effort would cost more than it could improve the call. When subagents are available, the isolation-critical pieces run as fresh subagents — the Phase 3 assessments, the Phase 4 derivations, the Phase 5 passes — because file-isolated sections written by one model have been seen to miss a future and a dissent that real isolation found; research, the model and the synthesis stay in the main context. Each piece gets a tight prompt with the files it needs, never the whole conversation; without subagents, approximate the isolation with files exactly as level-2.md describes and label it. If the record turns out to hold no numbers, build the robustness table and keep going — this level's value is the procedure, not the model. If the decision turns out atomic, the table is its options and the interaction check falls away; every other mechanism stays.
