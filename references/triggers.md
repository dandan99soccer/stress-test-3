# Triggers and gates for levels 3–6, and the offer wording

Read this with the level's files when a level 3, 4, 5 or 6 phrase arrives, or when a level 2+ pass is deciding what to offer next. SKILL.md holds the short form — the phrases, the rule that an explicit request always runs the level named, and what each level reuses from the one below. This file holds the gates in full, the fast forms, the pack choice at level 6, and the exact one-line offers. Nothing here relaxes SKILL.md; where the two differ, SKILL.md governs and the difference is a bug to report.

**Level 3 trigger.** One phrase — "level 3" (or "level three") — escalates past level 2 (loose on wording: "run level 3", "take it to level three"). It works up front or as a follow-up like the other triggers, and can escalate a finished level 2 — reuse that pass's grounding and options rather than redoing them.

Level 3 keeps an importance gate even when triggered, because it only pays on decisions that are both important and genuinely composite. Important: costly or slow to reverse, weeks of work or material money committed, customers, compliance, or someone's livelihood touched, or a precedent set that others will cite. Composite: it splits into component decisions that could each go differently. Show both parts on the `Gate:` line. If either fails, say which failed in one line and ask: level 2, or level 3 anyway? The trigger was consent to spend; on a failed gate, which level to spend it on goes back to the user.

**Level 4 trigger.** One phrase — "level 4" (or "level four") — runs the decision-quality pass (loose on wording: "run level 4", "take it to level four"; not "level 4 support" or any use about something else). It works up front or as a follow-up, and can escalate a finished level 2 or level 3, reusing their grounding, options and strategies. It is the level where decision quality comes before economy: spend follows the weakest element of the decision and stops only when more effort would cost more than it could still improve the call. Its one gate is importance (the level 3 test); the record does not need to hold numbers — where it holds none, the model is a robustness table and the criterion is which strategy fails in the fewest plausible futures. **An explicit level 4 request always runs level 4.** When the user names level 4, the gate does not stop the pass and does not trigger a question: run it, and if the gate fails, say in one line at the top which part failed and that level 4 may be overvalued for this question, then continue. Spending this level on a trivial decision buys confidence, not accuracy, so the warning says so plainly. The level opens with the intake described below, then runs without asking again.

**Level 5 trigger.** One phrase — "level 5" (or "level five") — runs the research-and-replication pass (loose on wording: "run level 5", "take it to level five"; not "level 5 support" or any use about something else). It works up front or as a follow-up to a finished level 4, whose ledger, table, objectives and packet it builds on; up front with no level 4 in the conversation, it runs level 4 first and continues. Its gate has three parts, all shown on the `Gate:` line: the decision is important (the level 3 test); a wrong call would be severe and hard to undo, or the level 4 packet's weakest element is information — the call still rests on claims whose provenance is thin or on questions nobody has researched; and there is time for the research to land before the decision is due — the pass finishes in one sitting and waits on no one's reply, and when the deadline is tighter than that it runs its fast form and says so. **An explicit level 5 request runs level 5.** When the gate fails, say in one line which part failed and what the level is likely to buy here — confidence rather than accuracy when importance fails, research that cannot be used in time when time fails — then continue into the intake, where "level 4 instead" or "stop" are answers the owner may give without being asked. This level adds planned research, a graded source for every load-bearing claim, inference where the record is silent, pre-registered disconfirmation, named-case base rates, two blind replications and the questions for the people who hold the answers, all compiled into one brief. It does not add volume. Where level 4's weakest element is frame or values, the cheaper fix is the owner's answer, and the intake asks for it. "Level 5 fast", "quick level 5", or a time limit given with the trigger ("level 5 in 20 minutes") runs the **fast form**: every phase, on the top questions only, with one replication. At either pace, research questions that need several lookups may go to parallel agents on Sonnet at a lower effort (`references/research.md` section 7). The judgment runs on Opus at xhigh, and a Fable agent adjudicates when the logic is tricky — the runs disagree, the call changed, or it rests on an inferred link (`references/level-5b.md`).

**Level 6 trigger.** One phrase — "level 6" (or "level six") — runs the forecasting pass on a domain pack (loose on wording: "run level 6", "take it to level six"; not "level 6 support" or any use about something else). It works up front or as a follow-up to a finished level 4 or 5, whose ledger, table, objectives and brief it reuses; up front with neither in the conversation, it runs level 5 first and continues. Its gate has four parts, all shown on the `Gate:` line: the decision is important (the level 3 test); a wrong call would be severe and hard to undo; the decision turns on uncertain future outcomes that can be stated with a resolution criterion and date; and there is time for the sittings, or the fast form is named. **An explicit level 6 request runs level 6.** When the gate fails, say in one line which part failed and what the level is likely to buy here, then continue into the intake, which opens with the pack line and asks the risk-attitude question. The pack is chosen in Phase 0 from the decision's object — `references/packs/equities.md` for a stock, an ADR, an ETF held as a position or a weight in a book; `references/packs/paid-search.md` with `paid-search-b.md` for a search-advertising account's budget, bid, structure, keyword, landing-page or brand-term decision (a draft until its first real run, which the Mode line says); `Pack: none (routes)` otherwise — and named on the Mode line. `references/level-6.md` is the procedure as it stands, with lines, the living runtime and the regime Monte Carlo deferred and the condition that revives each; the simulation it runs is the owner's requirement in `references/level-6-simulation.md`. "Level 6 fast" runs the fast form: the constraint call, the reference, three forecasters and one round on the top questions.

**When to offer the next level.** Without a trigger phrase, end level 1 with a one-line offer when any of these hold: the decision is expensive or slow to reverse, it commits to more than a few days of build, it sets a pricing or policy precedent, or being wrong costs real money, customer trust, or compliance standing. Word the offer so it teaches the trigger: *"Say 'level 2' to run the isolated version."* Each level may offer only the next one up, and only when that level's gate holds: level 2 may end with *"Say 'level 3' to split this into its component decisions and analyze each in isolation"*; level 3 may end with *"Say 'level 4' for the decision-quality pass: a short intake, objectives first, the outside view, every coherent strategy, three blind derivations and a scored decision record"*; level 4 may end with *"Say 'level 5' to research the load-bearing claims by several routes, search for the evidence against, and replicate the analysis blind"* — only when its packet's weakest element is information, or a wrong call would be severe and hard to undo. Level 5 may end with *"Say 'level 6' for the forecasting pass: the constraint call and the market reference first, one edge hunt, a calculated simulation and a policy table"* — only when the decision turns on forecastable outcomes with a resolution date. Level 1 never offers two levels up.
