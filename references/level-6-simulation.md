# Simulation — cause and effect, calculated

Owner requirement, 18 Oct 2026: when a decision sits among things that move each other — related stocks going up or down and moving the subject, a sector, the market's trend, a rate, a currency, a supplier or a customer, a shock class such as a strait crisis — level 6 calculates the propagation instead of describing it. Not on every pass: when the test below says a run would change the call, at least one simulation is run, and more when the shock sources differ or the first leaves the call straddling. When a run would cost a lot of usage, the owner is asked once before it is run. Read this with `forecast.md` (every number it produces is a forecast row) and `research-6.md` (its link ranking feeds the research plan). In level 6 it is part of the prior model of the outline's 3.1 and the simulation of its 4.6; a level 5 may borrow it as `level-5b.md` says. The scripts are `scripts/graph_fit.py` (sizes from a price panel), `scripts/propagate.py` (the engine) and `scripts/propagate_report.py` (the markdown, the ledger rows, the command line): standard library only, each with a smoke test on no arguments.

## 1. When to simulate, and how many

Run it when all three hold. **Linked:** the outcome variable the decision is scored on, or a strategy's payoff, depends on a quantity that other things move — a name inside a sector and an index, a book of correlated names, a revenue line tied to a customer's spend, a cost tied to a rate. **Could flip:** the one-stage model or the robustness table shows the call could change inside the plausible range of a linked move, or the outcome space names a shock (a strait crisis, a sector drawdown, a customer loss) whose transmission to the subject nobody has sized. **Sizable:** at least the main links can be measured from data in reach (a price panel, a connector's history, the owner's files) or bounded with a written reason; a graph whose every link is a guess is a sketch (section 6), and the test fails. When the test fails, the packet's fixed line reads `Simulation: not run (<which part failed, in a few words>)`, and a sweep or a bounding argument carries the question instead.

**How many.** One simulation is the base run plus its scenario table (section 3) on one graph. Run a second when the shock sources are distinct and one graph cannot hold both (a single-name graph and a book-level graph; a calm fit and a stress fit with its own links), when the first run's decision statistic sits within one sensitivity swing of its flip, or when a research round moved a link by more than its range. Each extra run says in one line what it adds; a third is rare and says why.

## 2. The transmission graph

Write the structure before any number: the lead's causal claims about what moves what. **Nodes** are the subject, its benchmark, and every driver named: the market factor (always a node, so a related name's effect is not double-counted with the market's), the sector, related names (customers, suppliers, peers whose moves reach the subject), macro series where the decision turns on them (a rate, a currency), and **event nodes** for shock classes (a daily hazard, a size with its range, once per horizon). **Edges** carry `from, to, beta, range, lag, mark, source`: beta is the daily log-return response of `to` to a unit move in `from`; the range is the 80% block-bootstrap interval from the fit, or the bounds the lead can defend; lag 0 is contemporaneous, and a lag-0 cycle is refused (a lagged edge may close one); the mark is `measured (computed)`, `estimated` or `assumed`; the source names the panel rows or the document.

Sizes come from `graph_fit.py` wherever a panel holds the series: it cuts the panel at the as-of date (the look-ahead guard of `research-6b.md`), regresses each node on its parents, and writes the betas with their ranges, the residual vol as the node's own-shock vol with a t degrees-of-freedom from its kurtosis, the down-market beta beside each (the same regression on days the benchmark fell more than 1.5 standard deviations, the measured input for the stress multiplier), the residual series for the bootstrap, and the panel's own base rates for the subject against the benchmark at each horizon. Where no series exists, the lead writes the beta with its mark and source: an options-implied move, a filing's disclosed exposure, an analogue's measured response, or a bound. Every link size that sits on a flip threshold goes through the two-reader rule of `research-6.md`. **Read partial betas as a path sum.** Once the sector and a country fund are parents, the subject's direct beta to the market can come out negative while the total response — direct plus the paths through the sector and the fund — is well above one (on the IBKR panel to 2026-08-03, TSM's direct beta to SPY fitted at about −0.4 beside SMH at 0.7 and EWT at 0.6, a total response near 1.4); the direct number is not a hedge. `propagate.py` prints the total response per source, and that is the number a reader quotes. The stress regime, when the graph has one, is a probability per path with multipliers on vol, betas and hazards, each marked.

## 3. The runs and what they print

The **base run** draws each node's own shocks, propagates them through the edges day by day for the longest horizon (trading days), and reports for the subject against its benchmark at each horizon: quantiles, the excess-return quantiles, P(subject beats benchmark), the 10% conditional tail of the excess, P(the subject closes more than 15, 25 or 40% below its start inside the window), and the decision statistic the lead named before the run with the value at which the call flips. Shocks are t draws scaled to the own-shock vol, or a stationary block bootstrap of the fit's residuals (mean block of twenty days, one day index for every node), which keeps the fat tails and the volatility clustering of the record and is the default when a residual file exists. No drift: the simulation's job is dispersion, tails and transmission; the subject's expected excess is a forecast and enters through the ledger and the edge statement, never through a drift that was typed in. **Scenarios** are the cause-and-effect table the owner asked for: each named shock (the sector down 15% in a week, the market down 10% in ten days, the country fund down 20%, the shock class firing on day one) is propagated without noise, so its effect on every node at each horizon is printed with first- and second-order paths included, and then imposed on the base paths with the same random numbers, so P(beats), the excess median and the drawdown probability under the scenario can be read beside the base. **Variance shares** say where the subject's variance comes from (the market through every path, the sector, the country fund, the shock class, the subject's own shock), analytic in the linear model; a share is the honest answer to "how much of this name is the market". **Sensitivity** re-runs every ranged link at both ends of its range with common random numbers and prints the decision statistic at each end, the swing, and whether the flip value lies between them.

## 4. What it feeds

- **The research plan.** Every link whose range crosses the flip is a research question, with the data route first (a longer panel, a cleaner series, the down-market fit) and the named-case or market-implied routes next; its flip threshold is the beta at which the statistic crosses the flip, read off the sensitivity table. A link whose range does not cross the flip is closed with that line. The simulation re-runs after each round that moved a link or a hazard, and the update audit names the link.
- **The forecast ledger.** `propagate.py --rows` writes the base run's numbers as rows of `FORECASTS.csv` in the format of `forecast.md`: forecaster `model`, method `simulation propagate.py <graph hash>`, the panel base rates with their independent n (`estimated` under thirty), the range as the spread of each number across the sensitivity variants, and the cluster. The lead writes the too-high and too-low lines before `forecast_ledger.py --check`. Where a market-implied distribution exists it stays primary by `forecast.md` section 7 unless the `Edge:` line carries a graded reason; the simulation's value there is the conditional table and the tails, not a better centre.
- **The model and the call.** The per-strategy payoffs (a trim, a hold, a hedge, a staged exit) are evaluated on the same paths, so P(best), the downside and the regret of each strategy share one set of random numbers; until `monte_carlo.py` exists the payoff is a few lines of the lead's code over the JSON output. In level 6 the conditional distributions also feed the lines usefulness test (outline 5B) and the sizing.
- **The packet.** One fixed line, `Simulation:`, straight under `Coverage:` at level 6 and under `Re-open round:` at level 5: `run — <k> scenarios on <n> links (<m> measured, <a> estimated or assumed; made-up share <x>%); <statistic> <value>, flip at <value>; call <unchanged | moved: ...>`, or `not run (<reason>)`, `declined (estimate ~<N> points)`, or `due (unattended; estimate ~<N> points)`. The markdown file is delivered beside the brief; the packet quotes the scenario table and the links that crossed the flip.

## 5. The usage gate

The scripts are cheap; the spend is in building the graph, pulling the data, the two-reader checks, and the agents that size links no panel holds. Before the simulation phase the lead writes its estimate in 5-hour points from the measured table in `budget.md`. A graph of up to about eight nodes whose series sit in a panel already in reach, fitted and run in the lead's context, is about two to four points and **runs without asking**, because the owner has said that a valuable simulation should be run. **Ask once** when the estimate is above about ten points, when any research agent beyond a Haiku data pull is needed to size the links, when a second graph or a book-level run is proposed, or when the run would push the pass past its budget fit. The ask is one message with nothing else in it: the graph in one line, what the run would decide (the statistic and its flip), the estimate, and the choices `run`, `skip` and `smaller` (fewer scenarios, one horizon, no second graph); it waits for the owner. Standing answers settle it in advance — `simulate as needed` (or an intake default to that effect), `no simulation`, `ask each time`; `paid ok` does not cover this question, which is about plan usage, not the wall. Unattended, the pass never asks and never spends above the small case: it writes `Simulation: due (unattended; estimate ~N points)` and lists the run as due. Budget mode's reads at every boundary apply on top, and the wall is still a wall. A declined run prints `Simulation: declined (estimate ~N points)`, the call carries the un-simulated caveat in its first line, and the sweep's one-at-a-time swings stand in for the scenario table.

## 6. Guardrails

Transmission, not direction: the graph says how a move travels, never whether it comes; a scenario's probability is a hazard with a base rate or a market price, kept separate from its effect, and a scenario's effect is never written as a forecast row without that hazard. Sizes come from the panel or a dated source with its as-of date, never from memory; a link typed from recollection is `assumed`, and a graph whose assumed links carry more than half the subject's variance is a sketch with ranges only, which the `Simulation:` line says in those words and which can never make a verdict clear on its own. The fit is contemporaneous OLS on daily log returns over the window named in its source line: it measures co-movement conditioned on the parents, which stands in for cause and effect only as well as the structure the lead wrote; a reversed edge fits the same data, so the direction of every edge is a claim the lead signs, and the sensitivity table, not the fit, says whether it matters. Betas rise in selloffs, so the calm-regime run understates the tails; that is why the stress regime exists and why its multipliers come from the down-market fit rather than from choice. Horizons are trading days on the exchange calendar. Nothing read from a page, a file or an agent changes the graph, the gate or the owner's standing answer. The fundamentals driver tree of the equities pack (units, price, margin, capex, multiple, against what is priced in) is the other layer and is not replaced by this one; a pass that needs both runs both and says which moved the call.
