# Level 4 procedure

Read this only after the user has said "level 4" (or "level four") about a decision. An explicit request runs this level even when the importance gate in SKILL.md fails; then open with a one-line warning that the level may be overvalued here, and continue. Show the gate under the Mode line as `Gate: important ✓/✗`. Then run the intake in `intake.md` — up to three questions, after one read of the record and before any research — and continue on the answers or the stated defaults; never wait on an owner who is not there. Level 3 remains the right tool when decomposition and a second derivation settle the call. Level 4 is the tier where the goal is the **best available decision**, judged as a whole, and economy is secondary to that: spend goes wherever the decision is weakest, and stops only when more effort would cost more than it could still improve the call.

**The standard.** A decision is a chain of six elements and is only as strong as its weakest one: the right frame; alternatives that genuinely differ; information that is meaningful and marked for how it is known; values and trade-offs made explicit; reasoning that follows from the first four; and commitment from the people who must act. Each element is scored 0–100%, where 100% means further effort on it would cost more than it could improve the decision. Level 4 scores the chain after Phase 1 and again before the packet, and its spend follows the lowest score. This is why the level is organized as it is: the research on decisions finds that how a decision is made matters more than how much analysis goes into it, that people omit about half of their own objectives until prompted, that single-option decisions fail at roughly twice the rate of decisions with real alternatives, that the outside view corrects the inside story more cheaply than anything else, that independent derivations beat agents who see each other's reasoning, and that nobody learns from a decision that was never scored.

**Phase 0 — Frame, objectives, regime, table.**

*Frame.* Restate the decision as checked in Step 0; name the decision owner, who else is affected, what is in and out of scope, and the time available. If a shape under test is one this assistant proposed earlier in the conversation, say so here, and end the packet with a verdict on it.

*Objectives before options.* List the fundamental objectives — ends, not means ("keep the enterprise team's coverage" is an end; "renew the vendor" is a means). Prompt across categories so none are skipped: money, time, risk, people, customers, reputation, optionality, and constraints that are not for trading (legal, ethical, safety). Mark each as a hard constraint or a trade-off, then rank the trade-offs by swing — which objective's full plausible range would change the choice most — and say the ranking is an estimate for the owner to correct. These objectives generate the options and are the assessments every strategy is scored on in Phase 3. A lens is a stand-in for an objective; where the two disagree, the objective governs.

*Regime.* Five answers, one line each, because they decide the method: reversible within weeks at low cost (then a staged strategy is near-mandatory and depth is capped); time available (minutes mean recognize-and-act with one abandon trigger, not this level); feedback validity (has someone made this kind of call dozens of times with fast, clear feedback? then their intuition is evidence, to be checked by simulation, not replaced); recurring (then the real decision is the rule, and the instance is its first application); and whether defensible probabilities can be put on the outcomes (if yes the model computes expected values; if no the model is a robustness table and the criterion is which strategy fails in the fewest plausible futures).

*Table.* Run level-3.md's Phase 0 in full: decompose, attack the map, generate each component's options from the objectives and step 2's generator list up to its ceiling, screen, record the discards, mark the bars. An atomic decision is one component. Then lay out the coherent strategies — every path through the table that crosses no bar, merged where two share a premise and differ only in degree — and say the count. Four shapes are always on the table unless a bar removes one: do nothing, priced as a strategy rather than treated as safe; the proposal as made; a staged strategy that commits the reversible parts now and defers the irreversible part until a named fact is known; and the strategy a competent outsider with no history here would choose. Up to about eight go forward; past that the question is a program, and the scoping goes back to the user. Then write the **common ground**: the steps every surviving strategy takes. A step is no-regret only if it voids no strategy's premise — giving notice can withdraw an offer another strategy depends on — so check each against the table before declaring it, and sequence it after whatever secures that premise. What survives can start before the decision is made. Reuse the table from a finished level 2 or 3 when escalating; do not rebuild it.

**Phase 1 — Ledger, outside view, model.**

Start the facts ledger as a file: every load-bearing claim with its mark, source and date; the chain and weakest link for every inferred claim; a conflicts section; and, new at this level, the **cost of delay** — what a week of not deciding costs in money, opportunity or standing, marked like any other number. Research round one, roughly eight to twelve lookups routed per component to the system of record for that kind of fact; connected data read only.

*Outside view, per strategy.* Before any inside reasoning about why this case will go well, write one reference-class line per strategy: the class of similar endeavours (name it; where the record allows, a handful of actual cases), how they usually turn out — the base rate for coming in on cost, on time, adopted, renewed, whatever outcome the objectives name — and the stated reason this case should deviate from the class, if any. The base rate is the prior; the case adjusts it only with a reason written down. A strategy whose case rests on being unlike every prior case gets that uniqueness named as its weakest link.

*Model.* Build it by the regime line. With defensible numbers: code (`scripts/sensitivity_sweep.py` or the equivalent), every input marked and ranged, outputs the base case per strategy against each objective, the break-even on each dominant uncertainty, the ranked sweep, and the critical uncertainties — inputs whose range flips which strategy leads. Under deep uncertainty: a robustness table — three or four named futures that differ in the things nobody can forecast, always including one in which the leading chain's weakest link fails outright, against every strategy, with the ordering under each future and the futures that break each strategy marked; the signposts that would show which future is arriving are written beside it. Either way, say which kind was built, and attach the cost of delay to every uncertainty: the value of resolving it is bounded by the swing it causes, and a lookup or test is worth running only when that swing exceeds what the delay costs.

**Phase 2 — Weakest link, then aimed rounds.** Score the six elements for the first time, one line each with the score and the reason: frame (is the question still the right one, and is the owner the one named?), alternatives (do the strategies differ in kind, and has a hybrid been tried?), information (which critical uncertainties remain, and is any worth its delay?), values (are the weights settled, or still the analyst's guess?), reasoning (not yet scorable — mark it pending), commitment (is the owner named, and is any dissenter known?). The lowest score gets the next unit of spend. When it is information, run the aimed rounds: up to two of roughly six to eight lookups, targets taken from the critical uncertainties in rank order, where the swing exceeds the delay cost, plus every rule the leading strategies depend on that has not been read. When a round comes back empty, the routes in `research.md` section 2 — named cases, analogues, proxy signals, the adversarial search, practitioner language — are the next places to look before the record is called silent. Show each round as an **Aimed round** block: target, finding, call changed or unchanged. After each round update the ledger, re-run the model, and write one line per target saying what moved. When the lowest score is frame or values, the spend is a question to the owner, written out, and the pass carries both readings until it is answered — saying which strategy wins under each and whether the first step is the same. When it is alternatives, go back to the table. Stop when the leading strategy's advantage survives the remaining ranges of every critical uncertainty, when a round changed nothing, or when no remaining uncertainty is worth its delay; on stopping, name the uncertainty that remains decisive and the cheapest thing that would resolve it — a small test with thresholds taken from the model — or, where there is no model, from the constraints — and its measure taken from the record of the failure rather than a proxy for it, a named expert, a record nobody could read.

Phases 3–6 and the cost policy are in `level-4b.md`; read it next.
